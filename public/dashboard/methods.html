Methods and limits

How the numbers on this site were made, how much to trust them, and what they cannot tell you.

What this is

A country-year panel of 217 economies (this includes territories, so “economy” is the honest word) and 11 indicators from the World Bank and the IMF, 1995 to 2024. It was downloaded on 2026-10-14, checked against written rules, reshaped and analysed. Everything on the site is read from static files built from saved results; nothing is calculated in your browser.

Questions asked in advance

Each hypothesis, and the result that would disprove it, was written down before the analysis. Rejected and inconclusive results are reported the same way as confirmed ones.

How to read the results

  • Results describe associations in one panel. Nothing here shows what caused what, and nothing is advice for policy or investment.
  • Thresholds for events (a recession, an inflation spike, a debt jump) are choices. The analysis was repeated with looser and stricter ones, and sensitive results are marked.
  • Rankings of economies are not offered: the data are uneven, and economies with little data are mostly small ones.
  • Forecast results come from a test that used only past information. No forecast for 2025 or later is shown, and these models are not fit for decisions about real places.
  • Some World Bank inflation values disagree with the IMF's series; the affected values are listed in the repository.

Claims behind this site

Only claims whose numbers were reproduced a second way (or traced row by row to the source) appear here. “Checked” in the register means exactly that, and the register names any part that was traced only.

C5

Economies with data in fewer than half the years are much smaller than the rest, and their income ranks are no lower: the effect is positive for seven of nine indicators, small and not significant for the other two, and significantly positive for unemployment

Caveat: Rank tests; income group is ordinal; the lacking group is small (8 to 46 economies); failing to find a difference does not prove there is none

Evidence: outputs/m0/bias.csv (27 tests, Holm-corrected)

Status: Checked (population effect for every indicator recomputed with a hand-written rank test, tests/test_independent_checks_m0.py); income effect (rank-biserial, p) and region effect (chi-square, Cramer's V) for all nine indicators recomputed by hand, tests/test_independent_checks_p6.py)

C6

Pooling economies and years can change a correlation's sign or size; 22 of 55 pairs change sign, 4 materially

Caveat: Association only; intervals resample economies (200 resamples); within-economy values are driven by shared trends

Evidence: outputs/m0/correlations.csv, notebook section 5

Status: Checked (pooled Spearman and within-economy correlation of all 55 pairs and the 22 sign flips recomputed with pandas, tests/test_independent_checks_m0.py; interval endpoints of the five largest pairs reproduced within 0.04 by a separate bootstrap over economies, tests/test_independent_checks_p6.py)

C7

Unusual year-on-year jumps are concentrated in 2009 and 2020-2022 (at least 1.5 times an average year)

C10

Using seven features in 2015-19, k-means groups beat a null made by shuffling each feature only slightly (silhouette 0.18 to 0.19 for four to six groups against a 95th percentile of 0.16 to 0.17), and beat a correlated normal cloud with the data's covariance by 0.01 to 0.02 (95th percentile 0.17 to 0.18)

Caveat: Weak structure by the usual silhouette reading (below 0.25). Four to six groups were tried with no adjustment for the search. Shuffling columns removes the correlations between features, so beating it does not separate discrete groups from a continuum. The correlated-normal null was added after the outside review; staying above it fits weak structure or non-normal data and does not show clusters

Evidence: outputs/m1/null.csv, selection.csv, outputs/review/gaussian_null.csv

Status: Checked (silhouette recomputed by hand in tests/test_independent_checks.py; the permutation null reproduced with a separate k-means run: 95th percentile within 0.012; the correlated-normal null reproduced with a separate run, tests/test_independent_checks_p6.py)

C11

The groups are not stable: mean ARI is 0.57 to 0.64 for k = 4 to 6, below the 0.80 bar, so hypothesis H1 is rejected

Caveat: 163 economies with complete data; 200 resamples of economies; one feature set and scaling; z-score scaling, imputed economies and three other seeds all give mean ARI of 0.52 to 0.70, still below 0.80 (`outputs/m1/extra_sensitivity.csv`)

Evidence: outputs/m1/selection.csv, run.json; docs/reports/02_m1_typology.html

Status: Checked (reproduced in 15 variant runs, all below 0.80)

C15

Under the pre-registered definition only 2008 is a global shock year for inflation spikes, holding 15% of 88 events, so hypothesis H2 is rejected

Caveat: Thresholds are arbitrary; 1,000 shuffles; Holm across 29 years; annual data; the verdict holds under five seeds (share 15% to 36%); the test assumes the years open to an economy are exchangeable. With 10,000 shuffles 1999 and 2022 are also global years and hold 36% together with 2008 (C32), still short of a majority

Evidence: outputs/m2/summary_main.csv, yearly_main.csv, run.json; docs/reports/03_m2_shocks.html

Status: Checked for the event catalogue (recomputed in SQL; 88 events, 13 in 2008); the shuffle test was reimplemented with separate random numbers and finds 2008 every time, tests/test_independent_checks_p6.py). Borderline years are seed-dependent and documented, as in C17

C16

H2 is rejected under loose (39% in global years 2008 and 2022) and tight (none) definitions too

Caveat: Which years count as global depends on the thresholds

Evidence: outputs/m2/summary_loose.csv, summary_tight.csv

Status: Checked for the event sets (all three threshold sets recomputed in SQL, tests/test_independent_checks_p3.py); the shuffle test on loose and tight thresholds was reimplemented and gives the same global years (2008 and 2022; none) and the 39% share, tests/test_independent_checks_p6.py)
